Orbit Bloom

artist statement - faria hossain · art 3041 · baruch ssep mission patch

what does your patch represent?

Orbit Bloom is a mission patch in which a flower and a solar system are the same shape. Eight petals are drawn as orbits, each carrying a small planet at its tip, all circling a bright center that is both the pistil and a sun. It represents curiosity growing into discover something small and that opens outward into space.

1. What symbols or imagery did you choose, and why?

I wanted one simple idea that still reads at a glance, so I combined two things that seem unrelated. The flower stands for growth and care. The orbit stands for motion and cycles Drawing each petal as an orbit lets one image say both. The thinner inner orbits echo the diagrams of atoms and planets that I associate with science from the very small to the very large.

The planets at the petal tips act like pollen drifting off a bloom, and the yellow center is a sun that holds the whole system together. The rounded-square shape feels like a window, and the lines are kept thin and precise so the patch looks drawn the way a scientific diagram is. For color I chose a deep midnight blue for the night sky and a warm pink for the petals, so the design is calm and a little hopeful. The lettering curves around the edge, “Baruch College” above and “SSEP Mission 2026” below

2. How does the design reflect Baruch’s participation in scientific research and discovery in space?

A flower is something that is planted, watched, and cared for over time. A student experiment is much the same it begins as a question, is looked after carefully, and is finally sent somewhere new to see what happens. The orbits tie that idea directly to space, and the sun at the center stands for the shared mission that everything revolves around.

The eight petals can also be read as many students and many contributions, each on its own path but circling the same center. Baruch is a college where people come from many places to work together, and I wanted the patch to show that research in space is a shared effort. The name and mission text keep the patch clearly tied to Baruch’s place in the program.

A.I. disclosure

Yes, AI tools were used in developing this design. I used Claude, to help build the web-based patch generator that I used to explore variations, including its controls, colors, and layout. I drew my design, picked the palette, decided on the default settings, and gave feedback through many rounds of changes, and I selected the final version myself.
