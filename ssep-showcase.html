Artist Statement

what does your patch represent?

Orbit Bloom is a mission patch in which a flower and a solar system share the same shape. Eight petals are drawn as orbits, each carrying a small planet at its tip, all circling a bright center that represents both the pistil of a flower and the sun. The design represents curiosity growing into discovery, with each new question opening the door to something beyond what we already know.

1. What symbols or imagery did you choose, and why?

I wanted one simple idea that still reads at a glance, so I combined two things that seem unrelated. The flower stands for growth and care. The orbit stands for cycles. Drawing each petal as an orbit lets one image say both. The thinner inner orbits echo the diagrams of planets.

The planets at the petal tips act like pollen, and the yellow center can be represented as a sun that holds the whole system together. The lines are kept thin and precise so the patch looks drawn the way a scientific diagram is. For color I chose a deep midnight blue for the night sky and a warm pink for the petals, so the design is calm and a little hopeful. The lettering curves around the edge, “Baruch College” above and “SSEP Mission 2026” below.

2. How does the design reflect Baruch’s participation in scientific research and discovery in space?

A flower represents growth, discovery, and the process of reaching toward something new. I wanted to connect that idea to Baruch’s participation in space research, where scientific curiosity leads to exploration and new discoveries. The orbits tie that idea directly to space, and the sun at the center represents the shared mission that everything revolves around.

The eight petals can also be seen as the many students and contributions. Baruch is a college where people come from many different backgrounds to learn and work together, and I wanted the patch to reflect how research in space is a collaborative effort. The name and mission text keep the patch clearly tied to Baruch’s place in the program.
